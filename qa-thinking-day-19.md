# QA Thinking — Day 19

## Testing a Market Mid-Migration: Applying FEW HICCUPPS to the EU's 15-Minute Settlement Rollout

Since October 2025, EU transmission system operators have been required to settle electricity imbalances every 15 minutes instead of the previous 30- or 60-minute blocks ([European Commission, EU electricity trading in day-ahead markets becomes more dynamic](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-electricity-trading-day-ahead-markets-becomes-more-dynamic-2025-10-01_en); [EPEX SPOT, 15-minute products in Market Coupling](https://www.epexspot.com/en/15-minute-products-market-coupling)). This isn't a feature release with a spec you can check off — it's a live, staggered migration where different bidding zones cross over on different dates, and the "correct" price for a given quarter-hour has never existed before. That makes it a genuinely hard oracle problem, and it's a good excuse to practice a specific technique rather than just admit "there's no expected result."

**The domain, briefly.** Balance Responsible Parties (BRPs) submit a schedule to the TSO for how much energy their portfolio will produce or consume in each settlement period. When real-time delivery deviates from that schedule, the BRP is in imbalance — and depending on whether their deviation makes the system's imbalance worse or better, they pay or receive the imbalance price for that period ([withthegrid, Day-Ahead and Imbalance Prices](https://withthegrid.com/energy-pricing-epex-day-ahead-and-imbalance-prices/); [gridX, Imbalance markets](https://www.gridx.ai/knowledge/imbalance-markets)). Shrinking the settlement period from 60 to 15 minutes means a BRP that used to get one averaged price per hour now gets four independent prices — which is the whole point: a solar ramp that spikes and crashes within a single hour used to get smoothed away, and now it doesn't.

**Why this breaks naive testing.** You can't regression-test this against last year's data, because last year's system didn't produce quarter-hourly prices. You can't fully trust a "golden" reference implementation, because every TSO is building its own. This is exactly the situation Michael Bolton describes needing an oracle heuristic for, rather than a single correct answer: FEW HICCUPPS — a set of consistency lenses (history, image, comparable products, claims, user expectations, product, purpose, statutes) you use to recognize a problem even without a ground truth ([DevelopSense, FEW HICCUPPS](https://developsense.com/blog/2012/07/few-hiccupps)).

Applied to the 15-minute migration, that looks like:

- **History consistency** — does the *shape* of the new quarter-hourly price curve for a given day still look like a plausible subdivision of what the old hourly price would have been? If the system just replicates the hourly value four times, that's not "more granular data," that's a silent bug wearing a new column format.
- **Comparable-product consistency** — cross-border market coupling means a zone still on 60-minute settlement is trading against a zone now on 15-minute settlement. Test that aggregating four quarter-hours back into an hour reconciles with what the coarser-grained neighbor reports — a mismatch here is where cross-zone settlement disputes start.
- **Claims consistency** — the regulation states each ISP must be *independently* priced based on real-time conditions in that quarter-hour. Test specifically for the shortcut failure mode: a system that internally still calculates on an hourly cycle and only reformats output at 15-minute resolution.
- **Purpose consistency** — the stated goal is better renewable integration. Pick a day with a sharp midday solar ramp and check whether the new granularity actually *reveals* a price swing that the old hourly average would have hidden. If it doesn't, the system isn't delivering on the reason the regulation exists, even if every field validates.
- **DST edge case, concretely** — a clock-change day used to be 23 or 25 hourly periods. At 15-minute resolution that's 92 or 100 periods, and off-by-one errors here directly misallocate real money to BRPs. This is the single highest-value test case in the whole migration and the easiest one to skip because it "only happens twice a year."

**The exercise:** Take a data pipeline or reporting feature you own that recently changed its unit of aggregation (time window, currency, geographic granularity, whatever). Pick two of the eight FEW HICCUPPS dimensions and write one test idea for each — not a test case, just the *question* the heuristic prompts. Notice which dimension surfaces an idea you wouldn't have generated by just checking the spec.
